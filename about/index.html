Model weights

I’m Juan. I’m a self-taught developer and founder. I got my start in Flash design, advertising, and marketing, went on to lead technology teams, and have also helped early-stage founders figure out what to build and get their product teams started.

Right now, I’m building LotIQ with my co-founder, working on AI agents for independent car dealerships. I’m also spending a lot of time learning how the hell these models work. Human behavior, neuroscience, and good experience design are usually somewhere in the mix.

This is where I share what I’m making, what I’m learning, and the things I’m still trying to figure out.

Training history

checkpoint-0001

How did it know?

One of my earliest memories is sitting in the middle of the back seat of my parents’ Montero, watching the turn signal. My dad would switch it on, make a turn, and it would switch itself off.

How did it know we’d turned?

I didn’t ask. I just kept watching, every time we were in the car, trying to figure it out.

checkpoint-0002

Little drawings on the internet

I was a few years into studying materials science and engineering when a friend showed me a tiny helicopter flying across his screen. It was pretty primitive, and he controlled it with the keyboard. Then he showed me how he’d coded it in Flash.

I kind of lost my shit.

I’d always been into computers and games, especially StarCraft. But this was the first time it clicked that I could take an idea in my head and turn it into something that actually worked. I wanted to use computers to create things.

I left a full scholarship at Universidad Simón Bolívar to pursue what was described to me as making “little drawings on the internet.” Still one of the best decisions I’ve made.

checkpoint-0003

Finding my way into the work

I grew up in Venezuela and moved back to the US in 2007 with $100 and no computer. I called my way through the phone book looking for work until I found some.

Those little drawings became a career in Flash design, advertising, and technology. I worked with Fortune 500 brands and eventually led technology teams at M8, including through its acquisition by Dentsu.

checkpoint-0004

The most Miami startup possible

In 2012, I co-founded NightPro. It was basically OpenTable for nightlife, which might be the most Miami startup possible. Tablelist acquired it, and the product became TablelistPro.

After NightPro, I co-founded CoachHQ. That one didn’t work out. I got cocky after a small exit and stopped doing what I’d done the first time around: immersing myself in what my customers actually did.

I started thinking I could just design the product they would want. I was wrong, and it cost us dearly.

checkpoint-0005

Helping other founders build

After CoachHQ, I started working with other founders who needed help growing their teams and setting up their product organizations. At Bitstop, I led product, design, and growth, helping get those teams in place. Later, I worked at Sion. Alongside that work, I spent a lot of time learning through Reforge.

I also became a partner at Sangre. The people I’d once worked for had become investors, and eventually we became partners.

I liked working with founders, but after a while I realized I missed building things myself. I wanted to be hands-on again.

checkpoint-0006

That feeling, again

By the time I tried Claude Code, I’d been away from coding for about six years. I’d barely used ChatGPT. Then I asked it to build something, and it actually built what I’d asked for.

For the first time in more than twenty years, I had that same feeling I’d had watching that little Flash helicopter. It felt like the distance between thinking of something and being able to make it had suddenly got a lot smaller.

I dove in. I was also struggling to get good at using these tools.

A few months later, I came across Anthropic’s “On the Biology of a Large Language Model.” The title caught my attention because I was already interested in neuroscience and behavioral biology. It turned out to be about mechanistic interpretability, and it gave me a glimpse of how much was happening inside these models.

I had no background in machine learning, but understanding the tool better seemed like a pretty good way to get better at using it. I’ve been spending as much time as I can learning and experimenting since.

Then an introduction led me to my current co-founder, and we started LotIQ. AI had made being hands-on feel possible again, and I wanted to keep building.

Current state — Still figuring things out

Human behavior, neuroscience, AI, history, and evolution keep giving me more questions. I’m also into training, nutrition, sports science, and genetics.

A few other parts of my life: I volunteered with Gaza Sky Geeks through Mercy Corps, and I’ve competed internationally in paintball, including at D1 level, across South America, Europe, and the US.

I tend to share the personal stuff too. It’s part of how I got here.
